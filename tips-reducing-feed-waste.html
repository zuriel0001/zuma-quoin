Six ways to cut feed waste on your farm

Feed is your biggest cost. Small changes at the feeder can save a surprising number of bags.

4 min readPublished 24 October 2026

On many farms a noticeable share of feed never reaches a bird’s stomach. It is flicked onto the floor, spoiled by damp, or eaten by rats and wild birds. Cutting that waste is one of the quickest ways to lower your cost per egg or per kilogram of meat.

1. Set feeders at the right height

The lip of the feeder should be level with the birds’ backs. Feeders set too low let birds scratch and flick feed out. Raise them as the birds grow.

2. Do not overfill feeders

Fill troughs only about one-third full. Birds pick through a full feeder and spill much more. Topping up little and often wastes less than filling to the brim.

3. Give enough feeder space

When there are too few feeders, birds push and fight, and spill feed in the process. Follow the feeder space guide for your type and age of bird, and spread feeders evenly.

4. Choose the right feed form

Pellets and crumbles are harder to flick out than mash, and each piece carries the full formula, so birds cannot pick out only what they like. Ask us whether a pelleted feed suits your flock.

5. Keep pests and wild birds out

Rats, mice and wild birds eat feed and spread disease. Close gaps in the house, keep the surroundings tidy and store feed properly. See our guide to storing feed so it stays fresh.

6. Measure what you use

Record how many bags you use each week and compare that with the number of birds and their production. A sudden rise in feed use without more eggs or growth usually means waste, pests or a health problem.

A quick daily check

  • Is there feed on the floor around the feeders?
  • Are any feeders broken, tilted or leaking?
  • Is any feed wet, mouldy or caked?
  • Are there signs of rats or wild birds?

This guide gives general advice for healthy flocks. Every farm is different, so for sick birds or health problems in your flock, speak to a veterinarian.
