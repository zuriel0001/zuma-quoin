Changing feeds without upsetting your flock

When and how to move birds from one feed to the next without a drop in intake.

3 min readPublished 24 October 2026

Birds are creatures of habit. A sudden change in the look, size, smell or taste of their feed can make them eat less for a few days, which slows growth or reduces egg production. Changing gradually avoids that dip.

When to change feed

Each feed is made for a stage of life, so changes are part of normal feeding:

  • Layers: chick starter, then grower, then layer feed at the point of lay.
  • Broilers: broiler starter, then broiler finisher until market.

See the life stage on each bag or spec sheet for the right ages. You may also change feed when switching from mash to pellets, or from one supplier to another.

How to change gradually

Mix the old and new feed over a few days, increasing the share of the new feed each time:

DaysOld feedNew feed
Days 1 to 275%25%
Days 3 to 450%50%
Days 5 to 625%75%
Day 7 onwards0%100%

Mix the feeds well so birds cannot pick out the old feed. If time is short, a three-day change (two-thirds old, then half and half, then two-thirds new) is still much better than switching overnight.

Tips for a smooth change

  • Avoid changing feed at the same time as other stresses such as vaccination, moving birds or very hot weather.
  • Order the next feed in good time so you never run out and have to switch suddenly.
  • Watch feed intake, water intake and droppings for the first week.

What to watch for

A small dip in intake for a day or two can be normal. If birds stop eating, droppings change a lot, or egg production drops sharply and does not recover within a week, check the feed for spoilage and contact us or your vet.

This guide gives general advice for healthy flocks. Every farm is different, so for sick birds or health problems in your flock, speak to a veterinarian.
