Feeding & storage

How to store poultry feed so it stays fresh

Damp, heat and pests ruin feed fast. Simple storage habits protect your birds and your money.

4 min readPublished 24 October 2026

Feed is the biggest cost on most poultry farms, and it starts to lose value the moment it is stored badly. Moisture, heat, sunlight, rats and insects all damage nutrients and can make feed unsafe. The good news is that a few simple habits keep feed fresh until the last scoop.

What spoils feed

  • Moisture. Damp feed grows mould, and some moulds produce mycotoxins that harm birds even in small amounts.
  • Heat and sunlight. These break down vitamins and fats, so the feed slowly loses its nutritional value.
  • Rodents, birds and insects. They eat feed, tear bags and contaminate what is left with droppings and disease.
  • Time. Even well-stored feed is best used within a few weeks of the production date on the bag.

Setting up a good feed store

  1. Keep bags off the floor. Stack them on pallets or planks at least 10 to 15 cm high so moisture cannot soak up from the ground.
  2. Keep bags away from walls. Leave a gap so air can move and you can check for pests behind the stack.
  3. Choose a dry, shaded, ventilated room. A roof that does not leak matters more than anything else.
  4. Store feed away from chemicals. Keep fuel, fertiliser, disinfectants and pesticides in a separate place.
  5. Control rodents. Seal gaps, keep the area around the store clear and set traps or bait stations safely.

Use the oldest bags first

Stack new deliveries behind or below older stock so the oldest bags are always used first. Writing the delivery date on each stack makes this easy for everyone on the farm. Buy in amounts you can use within a few weeks rather than stocking up for months.

Once a bag is open

  • Fold and close the top, or tip the feed into a clean bin with a tight lid.
  • Never pour old feed on top of new feed in a bin. Empty and clean it first.
  • Scoop feed with a clean, dry scoop.

Signs that feed has gone bad

Do not give birds feed that:

  • smells musty, sour or mouldy
  • has lumps, caking or visible mould
  • is wet, or has insects or webbing in it
  • has been chewed into by rats or mice

It is cheaper to throw away one spoiled bag than to lose egg production or birds. If you have a concern about a bag of our feed, keep the bag and note the batch code so we can trace it.

This guide gives general advice for healthy flocks. Every farm is different, so for sick birds or health problems in your flock, speak to a veterinarian.
