Strong eggshells start with the right feed

Cracked and thin-shelled eggs cost you money. Here is what hens need to lay strong shells.

4 min readPublished 24 October 2026

Every cracked, thin or soft-shelled egg is money lost. An eggshell is almost entirely calcium carbonate, and a laying hen needs a large amount of calcium every day to make one. Getting the nutrition right is the foundation of good shell quality.

Why calcium matters so much

A hen uses roughly 2 grams of calcium to build a single eggshell. Most of the shell is formed overnight, while she is resting. That calcium comes from her feed and from reserves in her bones. If her feed does not supply enough, she draws on those reserves, and over time both shells and bones become weaker.

That is why layer feed contains several times more calcium than grower feed.

Feed the right ration at the right age

  • Do not give layer feed to young pullets. The high calcium level can damage the kidneys of birds that are not yet laying.
  • Do not keep laying hens on grower feed. It does not contain enough calcium for shell formation.
  • Move birds to layer feed at the point of lay, as the first eggs appear.

Other nutrients that build the shell

  • Phosphorus works together with calcium. Too little or too much upsets the balance.
  • Vitamin D3 helps the hen absorb calcium from her feed.
  • Particle size of calcium matters too. Coarser limestone particles stay in the gut longer and release calcium overnight, when the shell is being made.

A complete layer feed is formulated to supply all of these in balance. Adding extra ingredients on the farm can upset that balance, so check with us before you mix anything into a complete feed.

Other causes of poor shells

  • Heat. Hot hens eat less and pant, which disturbs the balance in their blood needed for shell formation. See our guide to keeping birds productive in hot weather.
  • Age. Older hens lay larger eggs with thinner shells.
  • Disease. Some infections cause misshapen, rough or thin shells. Speak to a vet if shell quality drops suddenly.
  • Stress. Sudden noise, handling or changes to routine can affect laying.

Collection and handling

Some cracks happen after the egg is laid. Collect eggs at least twice a day, keep nest boxes clean with fresh nesting material, and handle eggs gently in clean trays. Fewer cracks at collection means more eggs sold.

This guide gives general advice for healthy flocks. Every farm is different, so for sick birds or health problems in your flock, speak to a veterinarian.
