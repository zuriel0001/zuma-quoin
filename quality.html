Process & quality

Quality you can trace, from grain to bag

Good feed is made, not mixed. Here is how we turn raw ingredients into consistent, safe poultry feed, and the checks we run along the way.

Note for the Zumo Quoin team: this page describes a typical feed mill process. Check each step against how your mill actually works, and add your certifications, before launch.

Our production process

Seven steps to every bag

  1. 01

    Raw material sourcing

    We buy maize, soybean meal, wheat bran, fish meal, limestone and other ingredients from approved suppliers.

    • Approved supplier list
    • Specifications agreed in advance
  2. 02

    Intake inspection & testing

    Every delivery is sampled on arrival. Loads that do not meet our standards are rejected before they enter the mill.

    • Moisture and visual quality
    • Protein and nutrient content
    • Mycotoxin (aflatoxin) screening
  3. 03

    Cleaning & grinding

    Grain is cleaned to remove dust and foreign material, then hammer-milled to the right particle size for each feed.

  4. 04

    Precise batching & mixing

    Ingredients, vitamins and minerals are weighed to the formula and mixed until every part of the batch is the same.

    • Weighed batching to formula
    • Timed mixing for an even blend
  5. 05

    Conditioning & pelleting

    For pellets and crumbles, mash is conditioned with steam and pressed through a die, which improves digestibility and reduces waste.

  6. 06

    Cooling, sampling & bagging

    Feed is cooled and dried to a safe moisture level, sampled for testing, then weighed into sealed, labelled bags.

    • Finished-feed samples retained
    • Batch code and date on every bag
  7. 07

    Storage & dispatch

    Bags are stored off the floor in a dry, pest-controlled warehouse and dispatched first-in, first-out so feed reaches you fresh.

Questions

Frequently asked

How should I store feed on the farm?

Keep bags on pallets, off the floor and away from walls, in a dry, cool and well-ventilated store. Protect them from rain, direct sun, rodents and insects, and use older bags first.

How long does feed keep?

For best results, use feed within [X] weeks of the production date printed on the bag. Feed that smells musty or shows mould should not be used.

What does the batch code on the bag mean?

The batch code identifies when and how the feed was made. If you ever have a question about a bag, quote the code and we can trace it back to its production run.

Can I switch feeds straight away?

Change feeds gradually over three to five days by mixing increasing amounts of the new feed with the old. This keeps intake steady and avoids stress on the flock.
